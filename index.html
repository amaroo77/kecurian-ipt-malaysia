Isu Kecurian dan Persepsi Keselamatan dalam Kalangan Pelajar Institusi Pengajian Tinggi di Malaysia

Theft and Perceptions of Safety among Higher Education Students in Malaysia: An Exploratory Survey of 190 Respondents

Abstrak

Kajian ini bertujuan meneroka pengalaman kecurian dan persepsi keselamatan dalam kalangan pelajar institusi pengajian tinggi (IPT) di Malaysia. Data diperoleh melalui soal selidik dalam talian yang diedarkan secara terbuka dan dijawab secara sukarela. Sebanyak 190 respons direkodkan antara 31 Oktober 2026 hingga 2 November 2026. Analisis deskriptif menunjukkan bahawa 71 responden (37.4%) melaporkan pernah menjadi mangsa kecurian di dalam kawasan universiti, manakala 119 (62.6%) tidak pernah mengalaminya. Daripada 69 jawapan lokasi yang boleh dianalisis, kolej kediaman atau asrama merupakan lokasi paling kerap dilaporkan (41; 59.4%). Faktor yang paling kerap dipilih ialah kecuaian pelajar menjaga barang peribadi (112 daripada 189 jawapan; 59.3%), diikuti kekurangan CCTV (111; 58.7%). Dalam analisis penerokaan, responden yang pernah menjadi mangsa melaporkan skor rasa selamat yang lebih rendah (min 2.59 berbanding 3.34; p < 0.001) dan lebih cenderung menganggap kecurian berlaku kerap atau sangat kerap (55.2% berbanding 24.1%; p < 0.001), manakala pilihan faktor penyumbang tidak berbeza secara bermakna antara kedua-dua kumpulan. Perkadaran mangsa tidak berbeza secara bermakna antara responden IPTA dan IPTS. Dapatan ini memberikan gambaran awal tentang pengalaman dan pandangan responden, tetapi tidak boleh dianggap sebagai anggaran prevalens kecurian bagi keseluruhan populasi pelajar IPT Malaysia kerana persampelan bukan kebarangkalian dan penyertaan kendiri digunakan. Kajian mencadangkan pengukuhan kawalan akses, pemantauan, rondaan keselamatan, penyimpanan barang yang selamat serta pendidikan kesedaran pelajar.

Kata kunci: kecurian; keselamatan kampus; institusi pengajian tinggi; persepsi pelajar; tinjauan; Malaysia

Abstract

This exploratory study examines experiences of theft and perceptions of safety among higher education students in Malaysia. Data were collected through an open online questionnaire completed voluntarily; 190 responses were recorded between 31 October and 2 November 2026. Seventy-one respondents (37.4%) reported having been a victim of theft on campus, while 119 (62.6%) had not. Among the 69 valid location responses, residential colleges or hostels were reported most often (41; 59.4%). The factors selected most frequently were students' carelessness with personal belongings (112 of 189 responses; 59.3%) and insufficient CCTV (111; 58.7%). In exploratory comparisons, respondents who had been victims reported lower safety scores (mean 2.59 vs 3.34; p < 0.001) and were more likely to perceive theft as frequent or very frequent (55.2% vs 24.1%; p < 0.001), whereas the factors selected did not differ meaningfully between the two groups. The proportion of victims did not differ significantly between public (IPTA) and private (IPTS) institutions. These findings give a preliminary picture of respondents' experiences and views, but they should not be treated as prevalence estimates for the Malaysian higher education student population because of non-probability sampling and self-selection. The study suggests strengthening access control, surveillance, security patrols, secure storage and student awareness.

Keywords: theft; campus security; higher education institutions; student perceptions; survey; Malaysia
